I don’t think Paulie Swan is cynical

11 November 2026 Pitchfork Paris Festival

follow pink iPod on Instagram @pink.i.pod

Paulie Swan that night, on the small stage of Les Disquaires, was surrounded by all his friends who came to trace back the steps of Amélie Poulain, eat, and see their friend playing. They injected me and the room with ease of life, which poured a new light on the self-proclaimed hesitant cynic I had talked to earlier. In May 68’, during the student revolt in France, a slogan came out “Sous les pavés, la plage”, “Under the rubble, the beach”. Could it be that under some of the strings and sadness of Paulie Swan, we might find lingering hope, or at least, lots of laughs?

Though it was harder to see the humour in his songwriting at first, this interview provided great insights and the mesh of inspirations and feelings which interline Paulie Swan’s music. This is my cue to speak about Joseph Nye. Despite his dark imperialist fantasies and my general distaste for the man, he might have been right about a thing or two about American soft power. I think he would have found great interest in the artist of our attention today; indeed, Paulie Swan was deeply inspired by many of the funny and sadder things happening overseas which he discovered online. Despite being very Scottish, you will not find the matching accent, “I watched too many cartoons as a kid” he told me. A voice of old times with very contemporary references, I was surprised to discover the Nietzsche references intermeshed so easily with South Park and deep-rooted youtube lore. And while his voice might sound older than he is (freshly 20), his own energy is young, and I think it’s specifically that energy which powers his delivery and performance.

Having studied film, Paulie is building his own myths through his songs, animating his own characters on his latest EP, and writing songs like fables, asking himself about his characters “What does the color of the sky look like in their world?”, and the color shifts you know, it bends, loops and changes in every song. I sincerely think there is much more awaiting Paulie Swan, like watching Swan Lake once because he told me he’s never seen the ballet before and I think that would be dope, but also much more for his career, poetry, and much more life.

INTERVIEW

Pink iPod: To start, I want to say I obviously stalked you thoroughly. And on January 2nd this year, you posted a video saying that you wanted to make a record, buy a keyboard, and have a show. I think you’ve done more than that. I want to know how you feel about it. You obviously had a very quick rise, do you feel pressured or you’re happy that you’ve gone above and beyond this goal?

PAULIE SWAN: Well, foremost I’m definitely happy. Obviously I’m doing something that I’ve always looked at from a distance and thought was somewhat impossible, but it’s working out, and it’s working out really well. I think there is definitely a lot of pressure that comes with it, but some of that is just being still relatively young and now doing some of my growing up in the public eye, however small a scale it might be. That’s coming with its own challenges, thought spirals, but I’m getting used to it, and at the very least it’s people being really nice to me, for the most part people who really care about the music and enjoy it a lot. I’m not waking up and getting a thousand people telling me they hate me and they don’t like me.

Pink iPod: (laughs) Yeah, I guess that would suck. Have you bought your keyboard?

PAULIE SWAN: I have. That was the way at the start of this year. That keyboard was paid for pretty much exclusively by Bandcamp sales from just that one song. It blew my mind. I remember staring at that. The song was up for a buck. It cost one buck and you could pay more if you wanted. I was staring at the money that was coming through, I was like, oh, I can’t touch this. This is blood money that I’m not supposed to work with. It comes from the internet. But I figured, oh, if I’m going to use it for anything, I’ll get the thing I said I would. So I got a keyboard. That’s been really fun for writing songs. It’s helped out with production because I can plug it into my computer. That was one of the first things I did.

Pink iPod: So you didn’t make a pack of the devil spending the bandcamp money?

PAULIE SWAN: No. Not as far as you should be concerned.

Pink iPod: I saw that you produce everything yourself, and it’s mostly a one-person job for now. Did you still find a music community in Edinburgh?

PAULIE SWAN: Oh yeah. I’ve been slowly escaping my dungeon of an apartment and going out to see people, and so I’ve gotten to make friends with some really cool musicians from Edinburgh and around, people who’ve moved back and forth. Shout out to Morgan Morris, he has a record coming out later in November. Gordon McGruer is real cool, Daisy Casemore is real cool, the Fatale guys are really cool. Check these people out, but they’re all very sweet folks and we get along.

Pink iPod: Amazing. I think you studied film and then you quit, right? How has film and, I think, animation inspired your work and your songwriting more specifically?

PAULIE SWAN: A lot. I think of things in characters and in worlds. When I’m writing these songs, they’re obviously all coming from me. I’m sure in some way they’re autobiographical just by virtue of me writing them. But often when a line comes to mind, I’m thinking of, oh, what kind of person would say this? That helps fuel the rest of the songwriting. It’s like, what kind of character would think this and feel this way about the world? What does the color of the sky look like in their world? And then that becomes the song. And I think I’m making a lot of subtle references to movies and TV shows in my work. And obviously with the recent record, I’ve done a lot of drawing and animation. It was really hard because I did it so inefficiently, but it’s really fun. I love cartoons. I’ve always loved cartoons.

Pink iPod: Do you have specific characters that come back into your work that you have sort of relationships with?

PAULIE SWAN: Wow. I think Shiner from the first record and Edie from the second record. In my head those songs are linked. In Shiner I’m singing about wanting to become the protagonist in Edie, who is this ultimate, fully self sufficient lover man or whatever, I guess. That is a very loosely inspired by a real person sort of thing that I’ve transformed into a character of my own to explore some fears and doubts that linger in my mind.

Pink iPod: In one of your songs, you directly get inspired by the French revolutionary in America, Rochambeau?

PAULIE SWAN: Okay, well, it was inspired by Rochambeau, as the name for rock paper scissors, and I looked that up and was like, oh, why is it called that? And there’s this whole guy who did these very wild and grand things, but I only knew his name because of rock paper scissors in South Park, so I was like, oh, that must be really funny. I’m picturing him rolling in his grave, really upset at me for not knowing who he is, and so I wrote about that.

Pink iPod: You mentioned you grew up on a lot of cartoons, but also on the internet. Do you think you could show me your YouTube feed right now and we can see what comes up?

PAULIE SWAN: I will. Okay, I’ll vet it first. I’m nervous (Pink Ipod covers her eyes).

Okay, it’s chill. All right. Right now I’ve been listening to a lot of Ween and a lot of Ween demos. So there’s some Ween, and Cory Kenshin Let’s Play from three years ago, The Onion news article satirical thing, Bonnie Prince Billy, Jacksfilms, more Ween, this page called Brewster’s Basement that does a lot of puppet stuff, film analysis, and then Tom Waits.

Pink iPod: I think that’s cool. I will vouch that this is a chronically online feed. Very interesting. Did you always have live performance in mind when you were writing these songs?

PAULIE SWAN: I think the songs across the first and second EPs were written all around the same time, which was this time last year, pretty much. I was writing them while going to a very local open mic. So I think I wrote them with performing by myself in mind. I knew that I had bigger visions for these songs, drums and horns and strings and things, but I also wanted them to be really strong on their own so I could go up by myself and play them.

Pink iPod: Because I think in that video from January 2nd, you mentioned that you want kind of a full band with you. Have you been able to do that once?

PAULIE SWAN: Not yet, but I have a drummer who I like a lot and who likes my music. His name is Joel. I love you, Joel. After that, if I can get a bassist together, then that’s already a three piece rock trio, and that’ll be enough for me to start.

Pink iPod: That’s great. So you’re on the search for a bassist?

PAULIE SWAN: Yes, but please don’t flood my inbox. I’ll figure it out.

Pink iPod: So I want to come back to Paulie Swan, because it’s not your last name, right?

PAULIE SWAN: No.

Pink iPod: Why did you choose that figure specifically?

PAULIE SWAN: I chose the name after most of these songs were written. I’ll be honest, some part of me just thought it sounded cool. I thought it had a nice ring to it. But also, it’s a beautiful animal. It’s a symbol in so many different places, in so many different ways. And if I’m going to be up there on stage singing these songs, and I want to have some layer of separation between myself and the person I am in the music, then it’s helpful maybe to have this big grand name that sounds like a mob boss or something. I think it’s fun for them.

Pink iPod: Have you ever seen Swan Lake?

PAULIE SWAN: No, like the ballet? No. I remember doing tuba in high school, and we had some Swan Lake excerpts in my book to learn, I think. That’s the closest I’ve got to Swan Lake.

Pink iPod: Focusing a bit more on the two EPs, I saw that you were describing your first EP as being sort of cyclical between emotional highs and lows, and the second one as being first an emotional high and then low. Why did you choose to end on a low note?

PAULIE SWAN: Shoot, man. I don’t know. I think I’m slowly realizing I’m a much more cynical person than I give off. I guess that comes through a lot of my music. I think it’s been helpful using these songs to explore some of the darker things within myself and brutally make fun of myself in a way that no one’s going to freak out about and check on me for. It’s really just the subject matter of the songs themselves. Once I put them together, that’s just the way it flowed best musically. I thought a lot about sequencing and I really wanted it to feel like a complete arc. And unfortunately, that’s how it went. But if you start the record on the third song and then loop it back to the start, then you can have your happy ending if you want.

Pink iPod: Absolutely. I was kind of wondering, because the second EP is called Swans Tomorrow? I was wondering what room hope has in your music in general, although you may tend for cynicism.

PAULIE SWAN: I wouldn’t be doing what I’m doing right now without hope. I wouldn’t get up in the morning without some semblance of hope. Sometimes hope is all we have. I think I talk about that a lot in Me Versus Mothra, and I think a lot of that comes through, or I hope comes through, in You’re Supposed to Get Old on the second EP. So I guess there is sort of a, that sounds so self important, a war within myself between hope and cynicism, maybe. I like to think the hope wins, or at least will win if it isn’t at any given moment.

Pink iPod: If you had all the money, all the budget in the world, all the time, what kind of project would you make? Musical or non-musical? It can be anything you want.

PAULIE SWAN: I would do an animated sitcom, probably. I would get to write it and hire all the writers and have a writers’ room, and I would get to voice-act a bunch of characters, and also compose some of the music. If I had all the budget in the world, I’d make a TV show, and it would probably be animated.

Pink iPod: My other question... Wait, I wanted to know first if you would put the little character that you animated in your recent clips?

PAULIE SWAN: Oh, in my show?

They could maybe show up in an Easter egg. You know The Itchy & Scratchy Show on The Simpsons? That cat and that rat killing each other? I’d probably have him over there on a show within the show. That’d be cute.

Pink iPod: And my final question is, what song would you play at your funeral?

PAULIE SWAN: I’m going to steal an answer from a movie called High Fidelity. He says Many Rivers to Cross by Jimmy Cliff. I’m going to say Many Rivers to Cross, but the Harry Nilsson version. Play that at my funeral.
