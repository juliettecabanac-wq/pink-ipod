Who is Pink iPod?

Pink iPod is a joke, a music journalist, a vintage piece of technology. Like many things on the internet, it will be whatever you want it to be. If you are still curious about the agitated cells behind these questionable design choices, here are some excerpts from an interview of Pink iPod by Girl Uninterrupted. Read the full version on their substack. Follow Pink iPod on Instagram: @pink.i.pod.

Girl Uninterrupted: If you were handed a wiped iPod tomorrow, what would be the first song or album that you would download?

Pink iPod: Well, first of all, I am on the quest for an iPod. But as Wiktor (a stranger I met at Rally) mentioned, I should never pay to get one. I agree, the iPod will find me.

For the first song, it would be “I can’t help but wonder where I’m Bound” by Tom Paxton. It’s extremely special to me and a lot of my friends. I asked them to perform it live for me at my funeral. I have an extremely planned funeral.

For the first album, I am extremely torn… I might choose a pink album to match. One of my favorite albums in the world is the Martha Wainwright self-titled. I saw a scene in big little lies where Shailene Woodley listens to Bloody Mother Fucking Asshole on wired headphones. I kind of want to recreate that.

Girl Uninterrupted: You are “pink” iPod. Is it important to you that your work aligns with femininity? (The “why?” is silent).

Pink iPod: The name comes from one of my first memories of music, in my room when I was 5, and I think I had stolen my mom’s Ipod. This wonderful piece of technology turned out to be the iPod mini 2004, in pink, as you might guess. I created the blog for a coding class and my website was due, so I quickly had to choose a name and this one felt right.

I started reflecting on the object I had chosen a while after I created the blog. As many little girls, I rejected pink when I was little. I was like : is choosing a pink iPod a way to take agency over my own femininity ? Or to heal my inner child or whatever ? Maybe in a way, but I do think in the end there is a bigger picture to it. I don’t wish to heal my femininity, I don’t believe in it. I completely reject any sort of essentialization of sex. Pink, which was used as a marketing tactic to sell femininity as softness and a general lack of danger, does not speak to me in that way and I don’t wish to.

At the same time, I don’t think “neutral” should become the masculine either. I don’t want my work to be feminine but I don’t want it not to be, in a sense. Probably why I gender my writing persona as “They”. I love pop, I love colors, I love joy, and the iPod represents that first and foremost. I think the iPod holds many of our pasts and I hope that way I can appeal to as wide an audience as possible, while cultivating inclusivity in who I interview and who I’m portraying. I hope all the people who don’t conform find a sweet spot in Pink iPod. I want to be the home where the friend group hangs out.

Girl Uninterrupted: What would you say is the common factor in all of the people you’ve interviewed so far? Is there one?

Pink iPod: They are all so talented! Truly, I will start with that. I am amazed by the amount of talent that is out there and that I was lucky enough to get a glimpse of. All the artists I interview, I am so convinced by their level of artistry.

Some other common patterns are :

  • As much as they can embrace it, they are all pretty tired of the social media thing.
  • They wish for more means and more time to make music.
  • Everything starts with community; a friend helping you shoot a cover, free guitar classes at your local record store, a small article in the local journal.
  • Free technology, free software, free shows are the backbone of our society. All the artists' introduction to making music was some free version of garage band or a guitar that was lended to them. Free access to music and music-making is more important than ever, that’s why culture should not be privately funded and owned.
