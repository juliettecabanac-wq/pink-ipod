The online kid who finally went outside : Lovebuzz

28 November 2026

follow pink iPod on Instagram @pink.i.pod

The first thing to say about Lovebuzz’s music, and the reason I wanted to feature him: it’s fun. The French American artist cares about the music and the craft, yet his songs are deliberately absurd, diaristic, open about missing his ex, among other longings. His tunes just stick, his live sessions are joyful and contagious, and I think the French scene really needed this. He belongs to a tiny emerging scene in Paris, where French rap’s new wave has taken on bright colours and indie music. I would also put in this scene Eliah and Lux but also Bulle, Garcon printemps, Arone, or the bigger a6el. For now I call it the colorful new gen but I’ll let them choose a name.

French or English, rap, indie or pop: like many in the scene, Lovebuzz doesn’t want to choose between the things that forged him and his sound. Half American, half French, he grew up in Lyon at an international school, raised on his mother’s CDs (Willie Nelson, Bob Dylan, a lot of Prince) and on the country music playing in the house, until a girl turned him towards Barbara and his French side.

He’s 20, doesn’t pretend otherwise, and makes music for anyone willing to bob their head along with him. On stage, he brings so much fun and energy. It’s fresh, it’s simple, it’s Lovebuzz! I think it’s this genuineness that makes him very promising. It’s music that doesn’t look at itself too much, but also music that is unafraid of itself. In many ways, Lovebuzz’s project is very generous. The image that came to mind: a teenager grabbing a few things, putting them in his bag, knocking on his friends’ doors and just going out. The online kid who finally chooses the outside!

Please meet Lovebuzz.

INTERVIEW

Pink iPod: My first question is always: what’s the first song you ever wrote?

LOVEBUZZ: Oh wow. The first song I ever wrote was called Ride My Bike, and it was about, I guess, riding my bike and spending time with my friends. I recorded it on my iPad, on GarageBand. I think I was 12.

Pink iPod: Did you discover GarageBand on your iPad?

LOVEBUZZ: I discovered GarageBand on my iPad just having fun at the time. And I pretty quickly knew I loved it, and I just did it every day, all the time.

Pink iPod: Was anybody else making music around you?

LOVEBUZZ: Well, my family: my two grandparents were musicians. They had a band and everything. So there was a sort of musical influence, but none of my friends really did anything.

Pink iPod: What was your grandparents’ band?

LOVEBUZZ: It was called The Old Men Boys. And they did folk music, country music, because they were American.

Pink iPod: Where is your family from in the US?

LOVEBUZZ: They’re from Illinois.

Pink iPod: I wanted to talk about all these genres that are in your sound. You’ve experimented with country, with, I think, almost anything possible: rap, pop. Because there is still a lot of unity to your sound, what do you identify as the common thread of all of it?

LOVEBUZZ: I guess, as time went on, especially through high school, I’ve learned to try to find what makes Lovebuzz, Lovebuzz. And I guess the word would be indie, in a way. I try to put variance on it, but I think it mostly stays indie. But I do love experimenting, and I wish we didn’t have to label it. But you kind of do.

Pink iPod: And in terms of lyrics, I feel like your more recent songs have experimented with humour even more. What are your inspirations for that? Because of course you do it in French and English, but I feel like that’s also kind of an American SNL thing.

LOVEBUZZ: I was talking about this this summer with a friend of mine: how it’s way harder to be kind of funny in French lyrics. And I’ve always loved the English language. You can be a little bit ironic and funny, and it doesn’t have to be comedy. So I’m trying to find a way to do that in French as well. I do love it when it sounds a little bit funny.

Pink iPod: It’s deadpan.

LOVEBUZZ: Yeah, exactly. And Bob Dylan, I feel like he’s really good at that. I feel like Bob Dylan kind of opened the door for me, and I realised I could try and do that. And then, I guess, so much country is also pretty funny.

Pink iPod: What kind of songs do you remember listening to as a kid, where you were like, oh, that’s titillating my brain, I would like to do that?

LOVEBUZZ: I was a big Queen fan when I was little. I love Freddie Mercury. I’d do dance shows for my parents and everything. But the music I really liked, where I was like, whoa, this is a whole song: the first song that kind of did that was Kids by MGMT. There’s something about that song. It’s like a pop song, it’s simple, but it’s genius at the same time. So I think that must be one of the first songs that made me wanna make music.

Pink iPod: I feel like you’re also evolving in a specific scene, I guess, here in Paris, but also within the broader underground scene. Do you feel like there are patterns or styles that you share with other people that kind of create this musical community? I’m thinking of Eliah, or Lux, who you work with.

LOVEBUZZ: I was kind of wondering if the scene was something I’d imagined, because it’s people I knew, or if it was really a thing. And I’ve heard a few people say, oh, there’s something sort of forming, which is really cool, I feel like. It kind of creates community and everything. I know I draw a lot from Eliah. He’s a big inspiration, and not just in terms of what he makes, but how he works and how he gets there. So when I make music with him a little bit, I’m like, I’ve learned so much. So I’m sure that in my music, without me even knowing it, it kind of gets in there. And Lux hasn’t really put out his own music yet, basically. But we often make music together, so it’s automatic. I feel like ever since he’s been helping me with songs, it’s elevated Lovebuzz.

Pink iPod: What is it about Eliah’s work and Lux’s work that inspires you, in terms of their methodology?

LOVEBUZZ: That’s a great question. I feel like Eliah doesn’t come from indie. He came from rap, more like trap and everything. Maybe not trap, more hardcore rap and everything. And so when I see him making a beat, he’s working on every little sound, how to make the guitar sound crazy. And I know, from where I’m at, I like to just have my song. So I’ll write the song first, and then I’ll go into the computer and start recording, and it might change. But he just goes into the computer, finds the song in there, and then he has a song. And it sounds great. Whereas Lux is kind of a mix of me and him. He’ll often have an idea for a song, but he also comes more from new gen, new wave, stuff like that. And he kind of does the indie sound as well. So I see how he loves to add synths and a lot of countermelodies. I’m more traditional, sort of. When it all mixes together, it’s really cool.

Pink iPod: Did working with him also introduce you to the sound of new wave and new gen, or is that something you were already interested in listening to?

LOVEBUZZ: I already liked it. I thought I knew a lot, but they knew way more, so I did discover a lot. And it’s always nice to discover more of a culture that you think you know, but actually don’t.

Pink iPod: Tell me if I’m wrong, because I didn’t do data on this, but it feels like your earlier songs are mostly in English, and you’ve integrated French, and it’s taken up more room recently. Can you tell me a bit about why?

LOVEBUZZ: It’s hard to tell why. I think, first of all, this has a lot to do with my ex, actually. Before I turned 16 or 17, I felt way more American. I had friends online from America, and my humour was more American. But then I met a girl who kind of introduced me to being French, in a way. And I think at that point, I was listening to so much French music: Barbara, all this stuff. And I was like, oh, there’s this whole side of me that I hadn’t discovered. So then I started writing more in French. And when I moved up to Paris, you hang out with French artists more, so I just gravitated towards that. But then this summer I went to New York, and all the songs I wrote were in English. So I think it probably has a lot to do with my environment.

Pink iPod: Where did you live throughout your life? What kind of environments did you grow up in?

LOVEBUZZ: I’d always lived in Lyon. My mom’s American, my dad’s French. But I went to this international school, so most of my friends were English or American, and there was this big melting pot of every country. There were some Spanish kids I was friends with, which was awesome, because at parties there was all this different music, which was really cool. But for some reason, musically, I shifted towards my mom’s taste in music, and it was way more American.

Pink iPod: What does your mom listen to? What are her big three?

LOVEBUZZ: Oh, she listens to so much music. She probably has Willie Nelson. She probably has Bob Dylan. I know she also loves... And among her CDs, I found a lot of Prince.

Pink iPod: You’ve talked about being inspired mostly by American media and humour. You have a song called Sarah Lynn. It’s from BoJack Horseman, right?

LOVEBUZZ: Yeah.

Pink iPod: Is there any piece of American media in particular that you’re a fan of, not necessarily inspired by, but just something that’s kind of part of your identity?

LOVEBUZZ: Yeah, I mean, I grew up watching Community. And I’ve always loved those super smart sitcoms, like Arrested Development as well. So I feel like those are the ones I really stuck with. BoJack was a part of my life. It’s really intense, but I don’t think I’d watch it anymore. I mean, it’s great. It’s just too much.

Pink iPod: Is there a character from any of these sitcoms, or from any media, that could inspire a song, like you did with Sarah Lynn?

LOVEBUZZ: Probably. I guess, well, this is way niche. This goes back to when I was watching Super Why. Do you know Super Why?

Pink iPod: No.

LOVEBUZZ: It’s on CBS or PBS Kids or whatever. It’s a kids’ show. And I used to watch it so much. And it did kind of stick with me, because these superheroes go into books. Maybe I’ll write about that one day.

Pink iPod: In terms of your inspirations, do you feel like there’s a genre you haven’t experimented with that you would like to have fun with?

LOVEBUZZ: I would love to get jazzier. But I feel like jazz is sort of like bossa nova, where people do it and somebody’s like, you’re not really doing it right, you’re not taking the time to really learn how to make bossa nova songs. And I feel like for jazz, I’d have to really get to a point where I’d studied it for a long time, and then I could write a cool jazz song. Not just bars and chords. So I’ll see. But maybe one day I’d like to get jazzy.

Pink iPod: Because you feel like the genres you’ve experimented with are genres you knew: you knew their history, you knew their context?

LOVEBUZZ: I feel like, yeah, I mean, you could always learn more, but I grew up with country. It was playing in the house. And I know a lot of people find country kind of silly, and it is a little bit silly, but I grew up with it, and I know enough about it. And then rap. And then hyperpop, I love hyperpop. I do feel like I have to feel a little bit legitimate to make it. If I don’t feel legitimate, then...

Pink iPod: Obviously you’re half American, and America is somewhere in your sound, and you have American flags and stuff like that. To what extent is that also a silly thing?

LOVEBUZZ: American politics, the American flag: it means so much bad, and some good. I mean, it’s a complicated history and everything. So I kind of do like the American flag as this post ironic thing. You know, there’s Nicki Minaj posters, and there’s the American flag: I kind of like it in that way. But also, I’ve been to America a lot, for my family there. And I see the two sides of America: big city America, and the small, super welcoming America, but maybe not so accepting America. There were some really nice people, but there’s these huge problems. So I feel like there’s some good in the American flag, in terms of what culture Americans brought out, all this music and everything. So it’s weird... it’s a bit silly, but it still has a lot of baggage.

Pink iPod: Were you a meme kid?

LOVEBUZZ: A little bit, yeah. More than I would have liked to be. I spent a lot of time on screens in my teen years, and I had an online partner and whatever. We spent a lot of time online, and it was a bit silly.

Pink iPod: How did that online partner thing end?

LOVEBUZZ: Oh, man. I got ghosted.

Pink iPod: You got ghosted! Fuck!

LOVEBUZZ: We spoke two years later, and they were like, oh my God, I’m so sorry.

Pink iPod: So at least it wasn’t some creepy man on the internet.

LOVEBUZZ: Oh no, no, no. They were legit.

Pink iPod: What do you think about the music scene in Paris?

LOVEBUZZ: See, it’s really interesting. I’m gonna sound like a hater. I thought the music scene was really bustling and everything, but then I went to New York this summer, and the music scene over there was crazy. It blew my mind. So coming back here, the first few days, I was like, man, we’re so behind. But now it’s getting better. I’m seeing what my friends are releasing, and it’s really cool, and there’s so many cool artists. So I’m still happy. I feel like growing up in Lyon, there was close to nothing for me in terms of indie and stuff like that. There’s a lot of cool rap things, but nothing like what I wanted to make. And there are things similar to me in Paris, so I love that I have that in this scene.

Pink iPod: And do you feel like the scene is welcoming to your sound?

LOVEBUZZ: I mean, it’s hard to know, because it’s still such a big city. I haven’t conquered Paris or anything yet. So I guess we’ll see.

Pink iPod: I guess my question is: do you see yourself evolving in the French scene, with the French audience, currently? Or do you feel like, to step up your career, you’d have to move?

LOVEBUZZ: That was a question I asked myself this summer. I do struggle with the bilingual thing. Because if I go speak French in America, nobody’s gonna understand me, but if I sing in English in France, not everybody’s gonna understand me either. So I think it’s sort of a struggle. There’s probably two ways my career could go, depending on where I live. The thing is, you can get bigger in America and still not be famous or anything. Whereas in France, it’s probably harder to get to that point, but once you’re there, you’re already more relevant, I guess.

Pink iPod: I think you’ve mainly put out singles so far. Why so? There are a lot of them.

LOVEBUZZ: Yeah, there’s a lot of singles. I just feel like I’ve done a few projects that I’ve never released, because they were so personal, and meant for a certain someone. I just didn’t feel like I could release them. But I also feel like I can’t just make an EP that’s a collection of three songs, like, here’s four songs I did. I need them to have some sort of theme. Not even a theme, but something tying them together. And that’s why the EP I’m preparing right now does kind of have that. So I’m glad I’ll finally have something to show for it. It’s been like five years.

Pink iPod: Oh, that’s cool. Can you tell me a bit more about the EP and what it’s about?

LOVEBUZZ: Basically, in high school, I was in love with this one person, and I wrote an album, and then another album. Yeah, I was really in love. But I really wanted to record the songs really well, and do them justice. And now that I have a sort of studio to record drums and everything, I’ll be picking the songs I think will show a timeline: being in love with that person, the end of the relationship, and then how life is afterwards. You can listen to the songs out of context, but I do like that if you listen to it all the way through, there’s a semblance of a story being told.

Pink iPod: What are some non-negotiables for you, in terms of not just creative freedom but freedom as an artist, for a contract or a label, or even management? What kind of contract would you like to have?

LOVEBUZZ: Well, in terms of artistic freedom, I spoke with one label once, and they were like, okay, well, we can do this, but you gotta stop with English. I was like, there’s no way. So I would need a hundred percent creative freedom. I can’t have anybody tell me, don’t do this part of your music, because that’s what I like about music: it’s kind of all over the place. Which I understand is also unattractive to some labels, because it’s harder to pinpoint who you’re gonna show it to. And in terms of freedom, I hear these stories of labels giving big advances, and then you’re left alone. I would hate that. I’d rather have less money injected into the project, but have it grow more organically, and also start touching money quicker. Just getting a lot of money at first and then waiting forever to actually start getting money...

Pink iPod: What’s the song you would play at your funeral?

LOVEBUZZ: Ooh, I thought about this this summer, and I couldn’t find one.

Pink iPod: What song would you play as you’re dying? Like, the last song you’d hear?

LOVEBUZZ: Idiot Wind by Bob Dylan. It’s a long song, so I get to live a little longer.
